# E-commerce Sales Forecasting & Inventory Analytics

**Data Analytics | Python, Pandas, Scikit-learn, SQL**

All data in this project is **SYNTHETIC** — generated by `analysis.py` (seed 42). No real sales data.

## What it does
Generates two years of daily e-commerce sales across 5 product categories (with trend, weekly and yearly seasonality, Black Friday / Christmas spikes and noise), aggregates revenue and units to daily and weekly level, evaluates demand forecasting models on a proper time-based holdout, forecasts the next 28 days of revenue, and turns the demand history into an inventory policy (safety stock and reorder points) per category. SQL equivalents for the aggregations and inventory inputs are in `sql/analysis.sql`.

## Method
- **Forecasting evaluation:** the last 56 days are held out (no shuffling — time order is preserved). A seasonal-naive baseline (same weekday last week) is compared against a Gradient Boosting regressor using calendar features (day of week, month, yearly seasonality) and lag / rolling features (lag 1, 7, 14; 7-day and 28-day rolling means). Holdout scoring is one-step-ahead using observed lags; MAE, RMSE and MAPE are reported for both models.
- **Forward forecast:** the Gradient Boosting model is retrained on the full history and rolled forward recursively for 28 days.
- **Inventory analytics:** from the last 90 days of daily unit demand per category — average daily demand, demand standard deviation, lead-time demand, safety stock and reorder point for a 7-day lead time at a 95% cycle service level (z = 1.65): safety stock = z × σ × √L, reorder point = d × L + safety stock.

## Results (actual run output, `results/summary.json`)
- Dataset: **730 days** (2024-01-01 to 2025-12-30) | **5 categories** | **3,650** day-category rows | **402,097 orders** | **710,571 units** | Total revenue: **£27,055,859.76**
- Forecast holdout (last 56 days):

| Model | MAE | RMSE | MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seasonal-naive (baseline) | £9,893.36 | £13,030.22 | 22.76% |
| Gradient Boosting | £5,212.57 | £7,073.98 | 12.17% |

- The ML model roughly **halves the baseline error** (MAPE 22.76% → 12.17%) — most of the gain comes from the trend and rolling-mean features the naive method cannot see.
